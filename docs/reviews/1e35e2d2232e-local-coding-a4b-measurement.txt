**Low L1: the binding output ceiling for this batch is the unchanged transport byte cap, not the stated
token allowance, and the published text does not quantify it.**
Locations: docs/evidence/t303-a4b-measurement-2026-11-11.md:16-22 and :28-30;
docs/evidence/coding-scoreboard.md:685-699.

What the retained data shows:

- For all 29 `chat.transport.limit` attempts, backend-slice.log ends with `srv stop: cancel task` followed
  by `stop processing: n_tokens = N, truncated = 0`.
- N minus the preflight input gives 16,202–17,778 generated tokens at cancellation, against allowances of
  22,998–29,579. For example, dev-001 stopped at 25,737 − 9,500 = 16,237 against 23,236.
- events.ndjson has no text events before the error, so the ceiling hit is most consistent with the
  stream/wire byte caps filling with reasoning deltas. Those caps are MAX_STREAM_BYTES 4 MiB at
  platforms/linux-inference/src/local_chat.rs:15 and MAX_WIRE 5 MiB at
  platforms/linux-inference/src/local_chat_transport.rs:30. The 1 MiB text cap is not consistent with
  text-free events.

What the documents say and omit:

- The documents truthfully say "All existing byte caps … remain".
- They report the 22,998–29,579 allowance as the output budget.
- They report 29 tasks as observed counters "unknown". They do not say that the larger timeouts turned the
  previous 29 timeouts into 29 byte-cap cancellations at about 16–18K tokens.

Nothing stated is false, and measurements are not gates, so this does not block. However, a reader cannot
tell from the scoreboard that the effective output budget was about 16–18K tokens.

Fix (future batches or T-309): state the effective byte ceilings next to the allowance. Also have the audit
record the backend `n_tokens` at cancellation, or name the transport sublimit in the error code, so that
limit refusals carry a generated-token position.
